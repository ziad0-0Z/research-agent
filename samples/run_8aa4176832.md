# Strategic Evaluation of Self-Hosted LLM Deployment for Financial Data Processing

## Executive summary
Self-hosting an LLM for financial data processing is only recommended if the bank has extremely high token volumes or specific data residency requirements that third-party providers cannot meet. While self-hosting keeps data within internal infrastructure, it does not exempt the bank from regulatory oversight and shifts the full burden of security, maintenance, and compliance to internal teams. The decision depends on whether the bank's internal engineering capacity and token volume justify the significant operational overhead and labor costs compared to managed services.

## Findings
1. Financial institutions remain fully responsible for customer data processed by AI models regardless of whether the infrastructure is self-hosted or provided by a third party. [ev6], [ev7] _(evidence: strong)_
2. Self-hosting does not provide immunity to security risks such as prompt injection, and it requires the bank to manage its own model supply chain, inference server patching, and access controls. [ev1], [ev2], [ev3], [ev5] _(evidence: strong)_
3. Self-hosting is generally more cost-effective than hosted APIs only when token volume reaches several billion per month, as labor costs for maintenance and setup are significant. [ev12], [ev13], [ev14] _(evidence: moderate)_
4. Maintaining a self-hosted model in production requires between 0.25 and 0.5 of a full-time engineer's ongoing effort, in addition to an initial setup period of approximately three weeks. [ev11], [ev16], [ev24] _(evidence: moderate)_
5. Dedicated GPU hosting offers more predictable latency than shared APIs, but requires the bank to manage hardware provisioning and scaling manually to avoid performance bottlenecks. [ev19], [ev21] _(evidence: weak)_

## Recommendation
Conduct a formal cost-benefit analysis comparing the bank's projected monthly token volume against the 1-3 billion token break-even threshold. If volume is below this threshold, prioritize managed private cloud AI services that meet data residency requirements. If self-hosting is pursued, ensure that the compliance and risk functions approve the deployment model and that internal security protocols for model supply chain and audit logging are fully established prior to implementation.

**Impact:** high

## Limitations
- The research lacks a direct comparison of specific enterprise-grade private cloud AI service offerings against the bank's current internal infrastructure capabilities.
- Data regarding the specific 'data residency' requirements for the bank's jurisdiction is not provided, making it difficult to determine if third-party providers are definitively excluded.
- The cost analysis relies on general industry benchmarks rather than the bank's specific operational costs or current token volume projections.
