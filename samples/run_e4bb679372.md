# Feasibility and Compliance Research Report: Self-Hosting LLMs for Bank Customer Financial Data

## Executive summary
Deploying a self-hosted LLM for internal customer financial data analysis is feasible from a technical and regulatory standpoint, provided that strict compliance, access controls, and ongoing engineering resources are established. While top open-weight models perform within 5 to 10 percent of hosted APIs on routine tasks like summarization and classification, they lag on complex reasoning. The decision depends heavily on securing approval from compliance and risk functions, as well as accounting for the total cost of ownership including infrastructure and engineering labor.

## Findings
1. Regulated financial institutions remain fully responsible for customer financial data processed by AI models, regardless of whether the model runs on in-house infrastructure or through a third party. [ev6] _(evidence: strong)_
2. Self-hosting AI models does not exempt institutions from obligations such as outsourcing risk assessments, data processing agreements, data residency confirmations, and supervisor notifications if material. [ev7] _(evidence: strong)_
3. For in-house AI deployments, institutions must demonstrate access controls, audit logging, model change management, and incident response. [ev8] _(evidence: strong)_
4. Decisions to transition customer financial data processing to a new AI deployment model require approval from the institution's compliance and risk functions prior to implementation. [ev9] _(evidence: strong)_
5. Running a single inference-class GPU 24/7 incurs baseline monthly costs for compute, excluding storage, networking, and monitoring, while larger 70B-class models require two to four GPUs. [ev1], [ev2], [ev3] _(evidence: moderate)_
6. The total cost of ownership for self-hosting encompasses compute, storage, networking, and engineering labor, requiring between 0.25 and 0.5 of a full-time engineer ongoing to maintain model reliability. [ev4], [ev5] _(evidence: strong)_
7. Top open-weight models score within 5 to 10 percent of strongest hosted models on summarisation, classification, and retrieval-augmented question answering. [ev10] _(evidence: strong)_
8. Open-weight models trail top hosted models by 15 to 25 percent on multi-step reasoning, complex coding, and long-context tasks, with models under 15 billion parameters showing the largest drops. [ev11], [ev12] _(evidence: strong)_

## Recommendation
Proceed with evaluating a self-hosted LLM pilot for routine tasks such as summarisation and classification, provided that compliance and risk functions approve the architecture, and sufficient engineering resources are allocated for ongoing maintenance and audit controls.

**Impact:** high

## Limitations
- Cost estimates rely on current on-demand GPU rental prices and may vary based on enterprise hardware procurement strategies.
- Specific performance benchmarks on proprietary financial datasets were not evaluated beyond general NLP tasks.
